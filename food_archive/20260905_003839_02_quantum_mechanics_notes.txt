Quantum Entanglement and Superposition

In quantum mechanics, entangled particles remain connected so that actions performed on one affect the other, even when separated by immense distances. Einstein famously referred to this phenomenon as "spooky action at a distance".

Superposition posits that a physical system exists partly in all possible states simultaneously until it is measured. Once measured or observed, the system's wavefunction collapses into a definite state.
